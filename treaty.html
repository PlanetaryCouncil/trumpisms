Billionaire Non-Proliferation Treaty A cap at one billion, a destination for everything above it, and a list of what the surplus actually buys.

Beyond one billion, life doesn't get any better. Additional money invested into planetary commons.

The first half of that sentence is a claim about diminishing returns: past a certain point another dollar changes nothing about a life — not health, not safety, not time, not the people who will sit at the funeral. The second half is a claim about where the same dollar does still change a life, and that half is measurable. The treaty below is the proposal. The list beneath it is the audit: every line is an intervention with a published cost per life saved or life changed, so the surplus can be argued about in lives rather than in percentages of a pledge.

The treaty

Seven articles. No party has signed it; nothing here is law, and no living person's assets are described. It is written the way arms-control instruments are written because that is the argument: concentrated private capital is treated as a stockpile with a verification problem, not as a virtue with a tax rate.

Article I

The cap

A ceiling of one billion US dollars in net worth per natural person, indexed to inflation and reviewed once a decade. Below it, nothing changes. There is no confiscation, no floor, no cap on income — only on accumulated stock held by one individual.

A billion is not chosen as a fair number. It is chosen as an obviously generous one: the argument survives being wrong by a factor of ten.

Article II

The surplus

Everything above the cap is committed to the planetary commons and disbursed within ten years of crossing it. A pledge is not a disbursement; an endowment that grows faster than it gives is not a disbursement either.

The ten-year clock exists because perpetual foundations convert giving into governance — the money stays, the power stays, and the deaths the money would have averted still happen on schedule.

Article III

The commons

Surplus goes to goods no one can be excluded from: disease elimination, clean air and water, a stable climate, an intact biosphere, open knowledge and the public institutions that hold them. Delivery runs through bodies accountable to the people served.

Naming rights are not a commons good. Neither is a wing, a prize, a chair, or a rocket.

Article IV

Non-proliferation

Holdings are counted at the beneficial owner: trusts, foundations, holding companies, family offices and control blocks in an operating firm all count against the natural person who controls them. Splitting a fortune across vehicles or relatives does not create a new person under the cap.

Every enrichment programme in history has claimed to be for civilian use.

Article V

Verification

A public register of holdings above the cap, audited annually by an independent body, with the beneficial-ownership chain published in full. Inspection is a condition of accession, not a courtesy extended afterwards.

Unverifiable compliance is the same thing as non-compliance, whatever the press release says.

Article VI

The measure

Success is reported in lives changed — deaths averted, sight restored, children not poisoned, households lifted — with the cost per unit published and the method open to challenge. Dollars committed is an input, not a result.

Every figure in the list below is an estimate with an error bar. Publishing the error bar is the point.

Article VII

Accession

Open for signature by any person, and for adoption by any state that prefers to legislate it. Unilateral compliance is valid and needs no counterparty: one signatory who disburses is worth more than a ratified instrument that nobody applies.

Non-proliferation regimes work when the holdouts become conspicuous. That is the whole enforcement mechanism on offer here.

How to read this

The rates are cost-effectiveness estimates, not audited outcomes. Most of the direct-delivery lines come from published charity evaluations that model deaths averted per dollar; the historic lines divide a programme's reported lives saved by its reported spend, which flatters them, because the cheap work happened first and the counterfactual is never clean. Policy-leverage lines are the least certain and the highest-variance: a tobacco tax or a lead-paint regulation can dwarf everything else on this page, or return nothing at all if the law does not pass. Where a line cannot honestly carry a forward rate it shows a dash rather than a number — a zero is a judgement, a dash is an admission.

Figures are rounded to one significant figure and were current as published by the linked source; check the source before quoting one. "Lives changed" mixes units on purpose — a death averted, an eye restored and a child not poisoned are not the same thing, and the list does not pretend they convert.

The cap is the arguable part

That a dollar buys more life in a bednet than in a second yacht is close to arithmetic. Where the ceiling sits, who counts a fortune, and whether a treaty is the right instrument at all — those are judgements. Bring the number you would set instead.
