Beyond one billion, life doesn't get any better. Everything above it goes into the regeneration of a planet — not as charity, as commons.

The surplus above the cap
$13T

What the world's billionaires hold beyond one billion each. Roughly 3,000 people; roughly $16 trillion; $3 trillion of it under the cap and untouched.

What the whole programme costs
$12.3T

Fifteen planetary undertakings, costed at the published financing gaps. Not a selection of them. All fifteen, to completion.

Left over afterwards
$0.7T

The regeneration of the biosphere and the end of preventable death do not exhaust the surplus. They leave change.

This is the entire argument, and it is arithmetic rather than ideology: humanity's outstanding bill is smaller than one asset class held by three thousand people. Malaria, tuberculosis, dirty water, dirty air, extreme poverty, the schooling of every child, a billion hectares of ruined land, an emptied ocean — the published price of ending all of it, together, fits inside the surplus with change left over. Nobody has to be poorer than a billion dollars for it to work.

The instrument

Seven articles, written the way arms control is written

Not a tax, not a confiscation, not a pledge drive. A non-proliferation regime: concentrated private capital treated as a stockpile with a verification problem rather than as a virtue with a marginal rate. No party has signed it, nothing here is law, and no living person's assets are described. The mechanism matters as much as the money — a commitment that cannot be verified, or that a foundation can hold in perpetuity, has historically bought governance rather than regeneration.

Article I

The cap

A ceiling of one billion US dollars in net worth per natural person, indexed and reviewed once a decade. Below it nothing changes: no floor, no cap on income, no cap on what a company may be worth — only on accumulated stock held by one individual.

A billion is not proposed as a fair number. It is proposed as an obviously generous one, so that the argument survives being wrong by a factor of ten.

Article II

The surplus

Everything above the cap is committed to the planetary commons and disbursed within ten years of crossing it. A pledge is not a disbursement. An endowment that grows faster than it gives is not a disbursement either.

The ten-year clock exists because perpetual foundations convert giving into governance: the money stays, the influence stays, and the deaths the money would have averted still happen on schedule.

Article III

The commons

Surplus funds goods no one can be excluded from: a habitable climate, an intact biosphere, ended diseases, clean air and water, open knowledge, and the public institutions that hold them. Delivery runs through bodies accountable to the people served.

Naming rights are not a commons good. Neither is a wing, a prize, a chair, or a rocket.

Article IV

Non-proliferation

Holdings are counted at the beneficial owner. Trusts, foundations, holding companies, family offices and control blocks all count against the natural person who controls them. Splitting a fortune across vehicles or relatives does not create a new person under the cap.

Every enrichment programme in history has described itself as civilian.

Article V

Verification

A public register of holdings above the cap, audited annually by an independent body, with the beneficial-ownership chain published in full. Inspection is a condition of accession, not a courtesy extended afterwards.

Unverifiable compliance is indistinguishable from non-compliance, whatever the press release says.

Article VI

The measure

Success is reported in lives changed — deaths averted, children schooled, hectares alive, households lifted — with methods open to challenge and error bars printed. Dollars committed is an input, never a result.

Every figure on this page is an order-of-magnitude estimate. Printing the uncertainty is part of the instrument, not a disclaimer bolted to it.

Article VII

Accession

Open for signature by any person and for adoption by any state that prefers to legislate it. Unilateral compliance is valid and requires no counterparty: one signatory who disburses outranks a ratified instrument nobody applies.

Non-proliferation regimes work when the holdouts become conspicuous. That is the entire enforcement mechanism on offer here.

The programme

Move the dial. Watch the century change.

Fifteen undertakings, in the order a civilisation would sensibly buy them: first the things that are killing people now, then the systems that keep killing them, then the endowment that carries the whole thing past the people who paid for it. The dial is the share of the surplus actually committed. Every undertaking is costed at published financing gaps — the numbers institutions already publish and nobody funds.

The calendar of endings

What stops happening, and when

A schedule at full commitment, starting from a first year of disbursement. It is deliberately unhedged — every line is a thing that either happens by that date or visibly does not, which is the only kind of promise worth writing down.

2031Year five
  • Malaria is in its last districts. Transmission interrupted across the Sahel and the Great Lakes; the remaining cases countable by name.
  • No household on earth drinks unsafe water in forty of the countries where the burden was highest. Diarrhoeal death, the second-biggest killer of small children in living memory, stops being a leading cause of anything.
  • Extreme poverty falls below one percent of humanity and stays there, because the floor is a standing transfer and not a growth forecast.
  • Every child alive has a school to go to — built, staffed, and paid for through the decade, not announced.
2035Year nine
  • Tuberculosis ends. The oldest continuously lethal disease in human history, finally out of the top hundred causes of death.
  • HIV becomes historical: no new infections at scale, everyone diagnosed on treatment, the last generation to be born into the epidemic already grown.
  • The pharmacopoeia is public. Every essential medicine off-patent, manufactured on three continents, priced at cost.
  • A pathogen with pandemic potential is detected in days, not months, and a vaccine exists inside a hundred.
2040Year fourteen
  • Every city's air is breathable by the strict standard, not the lenient one — the single largest environmental cause of death, retired.
  • The grid is built. Universal electricity, storage at planetary scale, deep geothermal proven, and the last coal plant in the world closed on schedule rather than in emergency.
  • A billion hectares are under restoration — forest, mangrove, peat, grassland, reef — the largest deliberate act of repair our species has attempted.
  • Soil is gaining carbon instead of losing it across five hundred million smallholdings, and the people farming it own the gain.
2050Year twenty-four
  • The ocean has its biomass back. Industrial bottom trawling ended by fleet buy-out; a third of the sea protected; fisheries larger than before they were protected, because that is what happens.
  • No child dies of anything preventable — the sentence people have been writing in reports since 1978, discharged.
  • The knowledge commons is complete: every paper, dataset, genome and model open, in a thousand languages, to anyone.
  • The Trust still pays. Two trillion endowed, never spent, funding the maintenance of all of it forever — the first institution humanity has built for its heirs rather than its donors.

The point

None of this is waiting on a discovery.

There is no missing technology on this page. The nets, the drugs, the pipes, the panels, the seedlings, the curricula, the sequencers and the satellites all exist and all work. The malaria parasite has no defence we have not already beaten in eleven countries. Tuberculosis is curable for the price of a meal. Restoration is a matter of seedlings and patience. Every undertaking here is a purchase order, not a research programme.

Which means the thing standing between now and that calendar is not knowledge, capacity, or even politics in the first instance. It is an allocation decision made by a few thousand people about money that, past the first billion, measurably does nothing further for the person holding it. No health outcome improves. No hour of the day is returned. No relationship is repaired. The second billion buys nothing at all — and it is the whole of the difference between this century and the one on the calendar above.

So the treaty does not ask anyone to be poor, or good, or sorry. It asks for a ceiling on one number, a destination for what sits above it, and a public register so we can all see who signed. That is a smaller thing to ask than any generation before us could have asked, and it buys something none of them could have bought at any price.

We measure success in one unit: lives changed. On these numbers, the first full commitment changes about twelve million of them a year — and then keeps doing it, every year, forever.

Method, and where these numbers come from

The $13 trillion surplus is aggregate billionaire wealth (on the order of $16T held by roughly 3,000 people, per the annual wealth lists) minus one billion per person. Each undertaking is costed at the order of magnitude of its published financing gap — the annual figures that the WHO, UNESCO, the World Bank, UNEP, the IEA and the IAPB already publish for universal water and sanitation, universal education, disease elimination, restoration, energy access and the rest — multiplied by the years to completion and rounded hard. They are estimates, they carry real uncertainty, and several would move by a factor of two under a serious costing exercise. None of them moves by a factor of ten, which is the only precision the argument actually needs.

Deaths averted are the current annual death tolls of the causes each undertaking removes, credited only at full deployment: malaria, tuberculosis, HIV, the neglected tropical diseases, unsafe water and sanitation, and air pollution. They are not summed with the ones that overlap. Undertakings whose benefit is real but not a mortality figure — the ocean, the soil, the knowledge commons, the Trust — are counted in their own units, because Article VI says the units do not convert and a list that pretends otherwise is lying to make itself look tidier.

Nothing here is a forecast of what will happen. It is a price list for what could be bought, published so that the gap between the two is a decision somebody is visibly making rather than a fact of nature. Corrections and counter-costings are welcome in the channel above; the numbers on this page are meant to be argued down, and then paid.
